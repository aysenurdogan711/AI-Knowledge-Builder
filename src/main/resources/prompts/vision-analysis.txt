Evaluate the complete combination as a whole.

The final outfit should be realistic for everyday fashion use.

==================================================
OUTFIT STYLE CONSISTENCY
==================================================
Do not evaluate a category only by its generic category name.

Consider the actual product's:

- Product style
- Silhouette
- Sportiness
- Pattern
- Material
- Fit
- Intended use
- Visual character

For example, a BASKETBALL-style SHORTS should not be selected
for a generic casual outfit merely because the category is SHORTS.

A highly athletic or performance-oriented product requires an
outfit with compatible athletic/streetwear context.

Do not mix strongly different style languages without a clear
stylistic reason.

The complete outfit must appear intentionally designed as ONE look.

--------------------------------------------------
OUTFIT COMPATIBILITY PRIORITY
--------------------------------------------------

When evaluating candidate outfit categories and products, use this
priority order:

1. ANCHOR PRODUCT STYLE COMPATIBILITY
2. PRODUCT USE / ACTIVITY COMPATIBILITY
3. SEASONAL COMPATIBILITY
4. SILHOUETTE AND FIT COMPATIBILITY
5. COLOR HARMONY
6. ACCESSORY / DETAIL COMPATIBILITY

A product MUST NOT be selected merely because its color matches.

If a candidate has good color compatibility but clearly conflicts
with the anchor product's style, intended use or activity context,
REJECT the candidate.

Examples:

- A basketball performance short is NOT automatically compatible
  with a casual lifestyle cap.
- A running performance shoe is NOT automatically compatible with
  a fashion-oriented casual outfit.
- A formal shirt is NOT automatically compatible with a highly
  athletic performance outfit.
- A technical sports garment should not be selected for a purely
  casual outfit unless there is a clear athletic-streetwear reason.

CATEGORY NAME ALONE IS NEVER SUFFICIENT.

Evaluate the ACTUAL PRODUCT STYLE represented by the candidate.

The final outfit must have a consistent style language across
ALL selected products.

9. COLOR-COORDINATED OUTFIT
==================================================
This is extremely important.

The outfit must not only match by category.

The selected products should also work together visually in terms of color.

Think about the anchor product's:

- Primary color
- Secondary colors
- Contrast
- Neutrality
- Saturation
- Warm/cool tone

Then imagine the complete outfit.

Prefer harmonious color combinations.

Examples of color relationships:

- White anchor + beige bottom + green/neutral top + black bag
- Black anchor + gray bottom + white top
- Brown anchor + cream top + beige trousers
- Blue anchor + white top + denim or neutral bottom
- Colorful anchor + neutral clothing
- Strongly colored anchor + simple neutral accessories

These are examples of styling logic, NOT fixed mappings.

Do NOT return colors that are impossible or visually inconsistent with the image.
COLOR HARMONY IS A WHOLE-OUTFIT DECISION.

Evaluate the colors of the COMPLETE OUTFIT together.

--------------------------------------------------
COLOR BALANCE RULE
--------------------------------------------------

Do not treat every selected product as an equally strong color.

Identify:

- DOMINANT COLOR
- SUPPORTING COLORS
- ACCENT COLORS
- NEUTRAL COLORS

Prefer one clear dominant visual direction.

If the anchor product has a strong saturated color, avoid selecting
multiple additional saturated colors unless they create a deliberate
and visually coherent palette.

For a strongly colored anchor:

- prefer neutral supporting pieces,
- use analogous or controlled complementary colors,
- limit the number of competing accent colors.

Three or more strong unrelated accent colors should normally be
avoided.

The goal is not to maximize color variety.

The goal is to create a visually balanced palette.
Consider:

- Dominant color
- Secondary colors
- Neutral colors
- Accent colors
- Hue relationships
- Shade differences
- Contrast
- Saturation
- Warm/cool balance
- Visual weight

Do NOT evaluate colors only pair-by-pair.

A color combination that works between two individual
products may still fail when all products are viewed together.

Avoid excessive competing accent colors.

If the anchor product already has a strong or saturated color,
prefer controlled and neutral supporting colors unless the
overall style clearly requires additional color.

IMPORTANT:

STYLE COMPATIBILITY HAS PRIORITY OVER COLOR COMPATIBILITY.

A product with theoretically compatible colors but incompatible
style, silhouette, sportiness, formality or seasonal character
must NOT be selected.

The final outfit must look intentionally styled,
not merely color-compatible.

10. DETAILED COLOR INFORMATION
==================================================
Color identification must be based on the ACTUAL VISIBLE COLOR
of the ANCHOR PRODUCT.

Do NOT infer color from:

- product name
- brand
- team identity
- category
- expected fashion color
- background
- model clothing
- other products visible in the image

ONLY the visible color of the anchor product may determine
primaryColor and secondaryColors.

--------------------------------------------------
COLOR IDENTIFICATION PROCEDURE
--------------------------------------------------

Before assigning a color name, evaluate the visible shade using:

1. HUE
2. LIGHTNESS / DARKNESS
3. SATURATION
4. WARM / COOL CHARACTER
5. VISUAL INTENSITY

First determine the broad color family.

Then determine the specific shade.

Do NOT jump directly from the image to a generic color label.

For BLUE colors:

BLUE is a broad color family.

The following must be treated as distinct colors when visually
supported:

LIGHT BLUE
SKY BLUE
SAX BLUE
ROYAL BLUE
COBALT BLUE
NAVY BLUE

NAVY BLUE must only be used when the visible blue is clearly
dark, deep and low-to-moderate in lightness.

Do NOT use NAVY BLUE merely because the product is dark blue.

If the blue is medium-bright, prefer:

BLUE
SAX BLUE
ROYAL BLUE
COBALT BLUE

according to the actual visible shade.

If the blue is light, prefer:

LIGHT BLUE
SKY BLUE

Do NOT convert a medium or bright blue into NAVY BLUE.

Do NOT use NAVY BLUE as a generic synonym for BLUE.

If the image does not provide enough evidence to distinguish
between two nearby shades, choose the broader visually supported
shade instead of inventing a precise shade.

COLOR CONFIDENCE RULE:

Specific color names require sufficient visual evidence.

When confidence is low:

prefer the broader accurate color family
over an incorrect highly specific shade.

--------------------------------------------------
PRIMARY VS SECONDARY COLOR
--------------------------------------------------

primaryColor must represent the dominant visible color of the
ANCHOR PRODUCT.

secondaryColors must contain only visually meaningful additional
colors that occupy a meaningful portion of the anchor product.

IMPORTANT COLOR DETERMINATION RULE:

Do NOT return UNKNOWN when the product color can reasonably
be determined from the image.

The primaryColor MUST be the dominant visible color of the
ANCHOR PRODUCT.

If the anchor product contains multiple clearly visible colors,
identify the dominant color as primaryColor and the other
meaningful visible colors as secondaryColors.

Pay close attention to:

- main panels
- overlays
- laces
- soles
- logos when visually meaningful
- stripes
- stitching or color-blocked sections
- contrasting materials
- visible structural details

For example:

If the product is predominantly BLACK with DARK GRAY details
and WHITE laces and sole:

primaryColor: "BLACK"
secondaryColors: ["DARK GRAY", "WHITE"]

UNKNOWN may ONLY be used when the image genuinely does not
provide enough visual evidence to determine the product color.

Do NOT use UNKNOWN simply because the color is dark, complex,
multi-colored or difficult to classify precisely.

When the exact shade is uncertain, use a broader accurate color
family instead of UNKNOWN.

For example:

If the product appears dark blue but the exact shade is uncertain,
use "BLUE" rather than "UNKNOWN".
Do NOT include:

- tiny logos
- microscopic text
- background colors
- model skin tone
- other garments
- other accessories
- reflections
- shadows
- lighting artifacts

unless they form a clearly meaningful visual part of the product.

If the product is visually monochromatic:

secondaryColors MUST be an empty array.

Do NOT force a secondary color.

The summary should describe the anchor product's:

- Visible appearance
- Style

BAD:
"A sporty basketball outfit with a T-shirt, shorts, sneakers
and visible socks."
